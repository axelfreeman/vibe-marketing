Short answer — no way. Machines will always be smarter than you and better at using language. If you’re given a single text and asked “machine or human,” you won’t be able to tell. If you see many texts at once, you’ll notice stylistic repetition. But professional prompt tuning eliminates even that.

One text — indistinguishable.

Imagine: you are given one page of text. No context, no author, no source. And you are asked: was this written by a human or a neural network? The correct answer: you won't know. Because modern neural networks command language better than 95% of people.

The neural network knows the rules of the Russian language mathematically. It does not make mistakes in cases, does not confuse "тся" and "ться," and does not lose its train of thought mid-paragraph. Humans make mistakes. But this is not an indicator—a professionally tuned neural network can deliberately add "human" errors to become indistinguishable.

Many texts — you'll see the pattern.

If you read 50 texts from the same neural network, you will notice repetitiveness. Favorite turns of phrase, paragraph structure, sentence length. A human’s style also repeats, but with greater variability. A neural network is slightly more “uniform.”

Signs of a novice: perfect punctuation, correct-length dashes, no typos, the words “innovative” and “unique” every other sentence. But a professional who tunes prompts removes these patterns within an hour. After tuning—the text is indistinguishable.

AI text detectors are useless.

There are services that "detect AI text." They work terribly. They show 80% AI on text written by a real person. And 20% AI on text from ChatGPT. Practical value — zero.
